What it fixes by itself

This is the part the map does not show, and the part worth knowing before reading the map's overlays. A sync never re-clusters the corpus: it decides, per changed section, with plain arithmetic.

  • a file you did not touch Nothing happens. Its section cannot move; nothing recomputes it.
  • a file you edited Re-embedded, then compared against its own topic as it was. Still {{ rule.tau }} similar or better, it stays and only its own brief is rewritten. Below that, the content genuinely left the topic and it is re-placed.
  • a new file Placed in the topic whose centre it is closest to, if that is {{ rule.tau }} or better. If nothing is close enough it is parked — and parked sections form a new topic of their own once there are enough of them to be one.
  • a topic past {{ rule.cap }} sections Splits itself. That cap is the summarization budget: above it, one brief cannot stay faithful to everything under it.
  • over {{ rule.misfit }}% misfits in one topic It sheds them, each to the topic that actually fits it. No one asks you.
  • a topic down to one section Dissolves; the survivor is re-placed by the same rule as a new file.
  • the whole tree drifting A full warm rebuild, diffed against what is stored — only the topics whose membership actually changed are re-summarised.
A few misfits are normal, not a defect

Topics are not spheres, so sections at the edge of one always lean slightly toward the neighbour. A freshly built tree measures around 3% and that is healthy. The repairs above fire on thresholds — {{ rule.misfit }}% within one topic — precisely so that the normal residue is left alone. What the map's overlays draw is that residue: what automation looked at and decided was not worth a rebuild. They are a second opinion you can act on, never a queue you are expected to clear.

The three overlays on the map

Lines the tree itself cannot draw. Each answers one question and suggests one repair.

  • misfiled (pull)

    Which sections sit in the wrong topic? The arc runs from the topic holding them to the one they actually resemble, and says how many. Repair: move them.

  • near-duplicates (proximity)

    Did the clustering split one subject into two? The arc joins two topics under the same parent that are nearly the same thing. Repair: merge them.

  • what a change touched (propagation)

    After a sync, which briefs did an edit force a rewrite of? A line runs from the topic whose content changed up to the highest brief the judge said no longer held. Nothing to repair — it is the receipt.

    On a tree that was built once and never re-synced there is nothing to draw, and the overlay turning on changes nothing. That is correct, not broken.

Questions

If it repairs itself, why can I edit anything by hand?

Because clustering has no opinion about what matters to you. It can tell that two topics are similar; it cannot know that one of them is the thing your team calls "billing" and deserves its own name. Every hand edit is pinned, which is what stops the next sync from undoing it — a correction that silently reverts is worse than no correction.

I changed one file. How much gets rewritten?

Its own topic's brief, always. Above that, the judge model is asked per level whether the change alters what a reader must know at that level, and the rewrite stops at the first level that says no. A typo stops immediately; a file that changed what a whole subsystem does can climb to the root. The what a change touched overlay and the Activity page both show where it actually stopped.

Why is my brief a question back to me instead of a summary?

A model was called in a context that made it answer the caller rather than summarise the material. Rewrite it by hand on the topic page — that pins it — or fix the cause and re-run. A reply that does not look like a brief is stored with a flag rather than dropped, because a flagged brief you can see beats a topic missing from the map.

Nothing happened when I re-ran a sync.

That is the design working. Condensations are keyed by content hash and briefs by their members' signature, so a re-run over unchanged sources makes zero model calls. If you expected work, check that the source actually fetched new commits on the Runs page.

What does a topic's "fit" number mean?

How close that section sits to the centre of the topic holding it. Low is not wrong — the edges of a topic are where the interesting material usually is — it is only worth looking at when the misfiled overlay says something else is closer.

Can I have more than one tree?

Yes. A tree is a name plus the sources it maps, so one database can hold a tree per product, per team, or per question. Make one in Settings; the bar at the top switches between them.

Words

topic
A group of related sections, or of other topics, plus a brief. The node of the tree.
section
About 10 KB of one file. What gets grouped, and what an agent reads when it explores.
condensation
What the first pass wrote about one section — identifiers verbatim, plus GOTCHA: lines. Cached by content, so an unchanged file is never condensed twice.
brief
A topic's summary, written from its condensations rather than from raw text. Reference lists are appended by code, never by the model.
passage
About 800 B. What recall injects. A section reassembles exactly from its passages.
tree
Topics all the way up to one root, over a named set of sources. A database can hold several.
stale
A brief that no longer matches what its topic holds — the members changed, or the prompt, or the model.
pin
A hand edit the pipeline must not undo. Pinning is what makes a correction survive the next run.
parked
A section ingested and embedded but in no topic yet. On the waiting list, not lost.
misfit
A section whose own topic's centre is not the nearest one. A few are normal.

Not used here: cluster is the algorithm's verb — what it makes is a topic. Chunk was an earlier name for a section.
